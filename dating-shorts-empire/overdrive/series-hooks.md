# 5 Recurring Series Formats — so the channel reads as a show

| # | Series | Naming convention | Ideas it absorbs | Spoken cold-open pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Red Flag of the Day** | "Red Flag of the Day #N: <flag>" | 1, 2, 4, 5, 12, 16, 18, 28 | "Today's red flag: ___. Here's how it got me." |
| 2 | **100 Dates Diaries** | "100 Dates Diaries: Date #N" | 3, 8, 10, 13, 27, 30 | "Date number ___ out of a hundred. This one hurt." |
| 3 | **Green Flag Friday** | "Green Flag Friday: <flag>" (post Fridays, always) | 9, 19, 20, 22, 28, 32 | "It's Friday. Here's a reason to keep hope." |
| 4 | **Text Check** | "Text Check #N" (screenshot-style beats, kinetic text heavy) | 14, 25, 26, 30 | "Would you send this text? Watch." |
| 5 | **Do This, Not That** | "Do This, Not That: <situation>" | 6, 11, 23, 24, 31, 33 | "Everyone does ___. Do ___ instead." |

Why series win on Shorts: viewers subscribe to *formats*, not topics; a numbered series manufactures the next-episode itch; and the swipe-feed treats consistent hooks as a recognizable signature. Rule: when a series episode outperforms channel median by 2×, the next 3 uploads include 2 episodes of that series.

Visual signatures (already in the brand kit): Red Flag of the Day = red pennant top-left corner badge; Green Flag Friday = green pennant; 100 Dates Diaries = tally-mark corner counter; Text Check = chat-bubble frame; Do This Not That = split ✗/✓ layout.
